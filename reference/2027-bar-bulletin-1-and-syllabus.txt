CONDUCT OF THE 2027 BAR EXAMINATIONS
      Modality, Schedule, Coverage, and Syllabus

       Pursuant to its constitutional power to promulgate rules concerning
admission to the practice of law, and in furtherance of its Strategic Plan for
Judicial Innovations 2022–2027, the Court, through the Office of the 2027
Bar Chairperson, issues the present Bulletin in accordance with the 2025
Amendments to Rule 138 of the Rules of Court.

I. Modality and Schedule

               The 2027 Bar Examinations will be held on September 5, 8, and
       12, 2027, at local testing centers throughout the country, and will be
       administered electronically on a secure assessment platform. Two
       subjects will be examined each day. The morning sessions run from
       8:00 a.m. to 12:00 noon; the afternoon sessions, from 2:00 to 6:00 p.m.1

II. Coverage and Cut-off Date

       Six subjects will be examined.2 They are assigned the following
days, sessions, and weights:

Day 1: September 5, 2027 (Sunday)

Morning Political and Public International Law     15%

Afternoon Commercial and Taxation Laws             20%

Day 2: September 8, 2027 (Wednesday)

Morning Civil Law and Land Titles and Deeds        20%

Afternoon Labor Law and Social Legislation         10%

Morning Criminal Law                                                                10%

Afternoon Remedial Law, Legal and Judicial Ethics, 25%

       As stated in the Bar syllabus, only laws, rules, and issuances in
force, and jurisprudence promulgated, as of June 30, 2026, are
examinable.3 As to jurisprudence, only the doctrines and dispositions
set forth in the main opinion are examinable. Computations, if required,
shall involve only basic arithmetic. In Criminal Law, examinees shall
not be required to perform numerical computations to determine the
specific periods of penalties expressed in years, months, or days.

III. Examination Format, Grading, and Examiners

               Each subject will consist of 20 essay problems. A problem
       consisting of a single question shall carry a weight of 5%. Where a
       problem consists of two questions, their combined weight shall be 5%.
       The questions will test what a beginning lawyer is expected to know
       and be able to do: understand the governing principles, apply them to
       the facts, and articulate the legal reasoning connecting the two. A
       weighted general average of 75% in all subjects is required to pass.4

               A committee composed of the Bar Chairperson and the Bar
       Examiners will conduct the examinations. Assigned to each subject are
       four examiners whose identities shall remain confidential until the
       official release of the examination results.5

                                   A Personal Message
                            from the 2027 Bar Chairperson

            The Bar is an important gate, but it is not the whole road. At the
    end of the day, know that it is a mere test, the likes of which you have
    taken multiple times throughout law school. You have done the heavy
    work and made the hard sacrifices to reach this moment.

            Your readiness for admission to the legal profession will be tested
    by the Bar, but it does not replace years of formation. When you sit in
    that exam room, recall the recitations that humbled you, the professors
    who corrected you, and the nights you kept working anyway.

            Remember that the Court is far from looking for those who can
    recall the most laws or rules verbatim, for a computer can do that. We
    are looking for human lawyers who are ethical, competent, worthy of
    public trust, and can aid the Court in administering meaningful justice.

            As you answer each question, remain calm, read carefully, reason
    plainly, and write what an ethical and competent lawyer would advise.
    That is all the exam will ask of you. Crucially, trust in your own
    preparation. Let the years behind you carry you forward. #BaRRR2027
